We receive cashmere items that have been worn for years without ever being washed — the owners terrified of causing damage — and we receive cashmere items that have been ruined by a single machine wash cycle.

The truth is that washing cashmere is straightforward once you understand one principle: cashmere is a **protein fibre** — the same molecular category as human hair. You wash cashmere the way you would wash a fine wool sweater or even your own hair: gently, with minimal agitation, in lukewarm water, with a pH-neutral cleanser. That is it. No special equipment required.

## Why Cashmere Pills

Pilling is the primary concern with cashmere, and understanding why it happens is the key to prevention. Cashmere fibres are extremely fine — roughly 14 to 19 microns in diameter, compared to 25 to 40 microns for regular wool. These fine fibres are soft precisely because they are thin and delicate. When subjected to mechanical friction — whether from wearing, washing, or drying — the surface fibres break and tangle into small balls.

The pilling you see on a cashmere sweater is not a sign of poor quality. It is a natural consequence of the fibre fineness. High-quality cashmere pills less because the fibres are longer (less surface breakage), but all cashmere will pill to some degree with wear.

The goal of proper cashmere care is to **minimise mechanical friction** at every stage — washing, drying, and storage.

## The Hand-Wash Method

Hand washing is the only recommended method for cashmere. Machine washing — even on a delicate cycle — subjects cashmere to tumbling friction that accelerates pilling and can cause shrinkage.

### Step 1: Fill a Basin

Fill a clean basin or sink with **lukewarm water (30 degrees Celsius / 86 degrees Fahrenheit)**. The water should feel neither warm nor cool to your touch — body temperature is the target.

Hot water damages cashmere in two ways: it causes the protein fibres to felt (interlock and shrink), and it strips the natural oils that keep the fibres soft.

### Step 2: Add Detergent

Add a **small amount of pH-neutral detergent** — a cashmere-specific wash, a wool detergent, or even a gentle baby shampoo. You need approximately one tablespoon per garment.

**Never use:** regular laundry detergent (too alkaline), fabric softener (coats fibres and blocks moisture absorption), or bleach (destroys protein fibres).

Stir the water gently to distribute the detergent.

### Step 3: Submerge and Soak

Place the cashmere garment in the water and gently press it down until fully submerged. **Do not agitate, scrub, or twist.** Simply let it soak for **ten minutes**.

The detergent needs time to surround and lift the body oils and soil from the fibres. Mechanical agitation is unnecessary and harmful — the soaking action does the cleaning.

### Step 4: Drain and Rinse

Drain the soapy water. Refill the basin with clean lukewarm water and submerge the garment again to rinse. Press gently through the water to release soap. Repeat with fresh water until no soap remains — typically two to three rinses.

**Never wring or twist cashmere.** The fibres have almost no elasticity, and wringing will permanently distort the garment's shape.

### Step 5: Remove Excess Water

Lay the cashmere garment flat on a **clean, dry towel**. Roll the towel up with the garment inside, pressing gently as you roll. The towel absorbs the water without stressing the fibres.

Unroll and transfer the garment to a second dry towel if it is still heavily saturated.

## Drying Cashmere

Drying is where most cashmere damage occurs. The combination of gravity (hanging) and heat (dryer) is devastating to wet cashmere.

### The Flat-Dry Method

1. **Lay the cashmere garment flat** on a dry towel or a mesh drying rack.
2. **Reshape the garment** while it is still damp — gently smooth the seams, adjust the neckline, and pull the body into its original dimensions.
3. **Allow to air dry completely** at room temperature. This typically takes twelve to twenty-four hours depending on humidity.
4. **Flip the garment once** halfway through drying to ensure even airflow on both sides.

### What Never to Do

- **Never hang wet cashmere** — the weight of the water stretches the fibres, creating permanent shoulder bumps and a distorted body
- **Never use a tumble dryer** — the heat and tumbling will felt the fibres, causing irreversible shrinkage and a rough, matted texture
- **Never dry in direct sunlight** — UV rays degrade cashmere fibres and can cause uneven fading

## Removing Wrinkles From Cashmere

Cashmere does not hold wrinkles the way cotton or linen does, but it may develop creases after washing or storage.

1. **Steam rather than iron.** Hold a garment steamer ten centimetres from the surface and steam the creased area. The moisture relaxes the fibres without contact damage.
2. If you must iron, use the **lowest heat setting** and place a **pressing cloth** (a thin cotton fabric) between the iron and the cashmere. Direct iron contact will scorch and flatten the fibres.
3. **Never iron directly** on cashmere — the heat will create a permanent shine mark and flatten the fibre loft.

## De-Pilling Cashmere

Even with perfect care, cashmere will develop some surface pilling with wear. This is normal and does not indicate poor quality.

1. **Lay the garment flat** on a hard surface.
2. Use a **cashmere comb** (preferred) or a **fabric shaver** on the lowest setting.
3. **Gently comb in one direction** across the pilled area. The comb catches and removes the tangled surface fibres without cutting the base fabric.
4. **Brush off the removed pills** and inspect.

Do this every three to five wears to maintain a smooth surface. Removing pills regularly prevents them from growing larger and more entangled.

## Storing Cashmere

Proper storage extends cashmere's life dramatically. Poor storage causes moth damage, moisture buildup, and permanent creasing.

### The Fold-and-Store Method

1. **Wash or refresh** the cashmere before storing — moths are attracted to body oils and food residue on unwashed garments.
2. **Fold the garment** neatly — do not hang. Hanging stretches cashmere's soft fibres.
3. **Wrap in a breathable cotton storage bag** or acid-free tissue paper. Plastic bags trap moisture and promote mildew.
4. **Add cedar blocks or cedar oil sachets** to the storage area. Cedar is a natural moth deterrent that does not use chemical pesticides. Place the cedar directly in contact with or very close to the stored garments.
5. **Store in a cool, dry, dark location.** Heat and light degrade cashmere fibres over time.

### Cedar vs. Mothballs

**Never use mothballs with cashmere.** Mothballs contain naphthalene or paradichlorobenzene — harsh chemicals that leave a persistent odour and can damage delicate fibres. Cedar is equally effective as a moth deterrent without the chemical risks.

If cedar is unavailable, **lavender sachets** provide a pleasant alternative with mild moth-repelling properties.

## Reviving Stiff or Flat Cashmere

Over time, cashmere can lose its softness and become slightly stiff or flat. This is usually caused by detergent residue or mineral buildup from hard water.

1. **Soak the garment in lukewarm water with a splash of white vinegar** (one tablespoon per litre) for fifteen minutes. The acid dissolves mineral and detergent residue without coating the fibres.
2. Rinse thoroughly in clean lukewarm water.
3. Lay flat to dry. The garment should feel noticeably softer.

Repeat every six to twelve months for frequently worn cashmere.

## Cashmere in Nairobi's Climate

Nairobi's relatively cool, dry climate is favourable for cashmere storage — lower humidity means less risk of mildew, and cooler temperatures reduce moth activity. However, the dust in some Nairobi neighbourhoods can accumulate on stored cashmere. Store cashmere in breathable bags or wrapped in tissue paper to protect from dust while allowing air circulation.

During Nairobi's rainy seasons, ensure stored cashmere is in a dry location with good air circulation. Brief exposure to damp conditions can promote mildew growth in protein fibres.
