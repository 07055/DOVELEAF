## Why Grass Stains Behave Like Fabric Dye

Grass is not surface soil; it is a complex biochemical cocktail composed of:

1. **Chlorophyll A and B:** The green photosynthetic pigments that give plants their vibrant color. Structurally, chlorophyll closely resembles phthalocyanine — an industrial pigment used in synthetic fabric dyes.
2. **Carotenoids and Xanthophylls:** Natural yellow and orange organic compounds that bind tightly to cellulose.
3. **Plant Proteins & Cellulose Lipids:** Sticky organic matter that mechanically cements the pigment directly into the hollow core of cotton fibres.

When grass is crushed against fabric under pressure (such as a slide on the sports field), plant cell walls rupture, injecting liquid chlorophyll deep into the porous fiber matrix.

---

## The Fatal Mistakes of Home Treatment

* **Applying Alkaline Bar Soap Directly:** Many home remedies suggest rubbing a harsh alkaline bar soap directly onto a grass stain. **This sets the stain permanently.** Alkalinity chemically cross-links chlorophyll with natural cellulose, transforming the green mark into a permanent dye.
* **Washing in Hot Water:** Hot water denatures the plant proteins, cooking them into the yarn core and locking the pigment beneath a hardened protein shield.
* **Vigorous Friction:** Scrubbing with a stiff brush abrades the fabric yarn, leaving a faded or damaged spot even if the green hue is partially reduced.

---

## Safe First-Aid for Stained Garments

If a garment suffers a fresh grass stain:

1. **Keep Heat Away:** Never expose the stain to warm or hot water, direct sun drying, or an iron.
2. **Sponge with Cold Water & Liquid Enzyme Detergent:** Gently dab the spot with cold water and an enzyme detergent containing cellulase and protease.
3. **Alcohol Solubilization for Washable Fabrics:** Because chlorophyll is soluble in alcohol rather than water, dabbing with a 70% isopropyl alcohol solution on a white cloth helps dissolve the green resin without fiber distortion.
