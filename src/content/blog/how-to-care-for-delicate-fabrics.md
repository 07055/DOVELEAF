## 1. Silk: Nairobi's Favourite Luxury Fabric

Silk is everywhere in Nairobi's fashion scene — from the silk-blend kitenge wraps sold in Village Market boutiques to the pure silk blouses worn by professionals in Westlands offices and the silk-lined suits favoured by the city's corporate class. It is also one of the most unforgiving fabrics when it comes to improper care.

### Understanding Silk's Vulnerability

Silk is a natural protein fibre produced by silkworms. Its molecular structure gives it that unmistakable lustre and drape, but it also makes silk extremely sensitive to:

- **Heat** — temperatures above 30°C begin denaturing silk proteins, causing the fabric to become dull and brittle
- **Alkaline detergents** — standard laundry detergents have a pH of 9–12; silk requires pH-neutral cleaners (pH 7)
- **Sunlight** — UV radiation breaks down silk fibres and fades dyes, sometimes after just a few hours of direct exposure
- **Chlorine** — Nairobi's chlorinated tap water can discolour silk in a single soak
- **Friction** — wet silk loses up to 20% of its strength; rubbing or wringing wet silk causes permanent damage

### At-Home Silk Care

**Washing:**

1. Always hand-wash silk. Never put it in a machine, even on the delicate cycle.
2. Use lukewarm water (30°C maximum — test with your elbow, it should feel neither warm nor cool).
3. Use a pH-neutral detergent. Baby shampoo works well and costs less than specialist silk wash. Brands like Cetaphil cleanser diluted in water also work effectively.
4. Submerge the garment and gently press it through the water for two to three minutes. Never scrub, twist, or wring.
5. Rinse in cool, clean water. If your tap water is heavily chlorinated, use filtered or bottled water for the final rinse.

**Drying:**

1. Lift the garment out of the water without pulling — hold it by the seams.
2. Lay it flat on a clean, dry towel. Roll the towel up with the silk inside, pressing gently to absorb excess water.
3. Unroll and lay flat on a fresh dry towel. Reshape to the garment's original dimensions.
4. Dry in a shaded, well-ventilated area. Never in direct Nairobi sunlight, never on a hanger (water weight stretches silk), and never near a heater or air conditioner vent.

**Ironing:**

1. Iron silk while it is still slightly damp, on the lowest heat setting (the silk or synthetic setting on your iron).
2. Always iron on the reverse side of the fabric to prevent shine marks.
3. Place a clean, white cotton cloth (a pressing cloth) between the iron and the silk.
4. Keep the iron moving — never let it rest in one spot.

### Silk Storage in Nairobi

Nairobi's humidity fluctuates significantly between seasons. During the wet months (March–May and October–December), silk stored in wardrobes is vulnerable to mould.

- Store silk in breathable cotton garment bags, never in plastic
- Add silica gel packets or cedar blocks to absorb moisture
- Avoid storing silk in direct contact with wood, as natural tannins in wood can transfer to fabric
- Never store silk in direct sunlight — even through a window

### When to Take Silk to a Professional

- Before storing silk for an extended period (pre-storage cleaning prevents invisible stains from setting)
- After any spill or stain that does not lift with the hand-washing method above
- For silk garments with embellishments, embroidery, or beading
- For silk wedding attire or formal wear

## 2. Leather Jackets and Garments

Nairobi's climate — warm days with cool evenings, especially in the Karen, Lavington, and Lang'ata areas — makes leather jackets a wardrobe staple. But leather is a completely different category of fabric care, and the mistakes people make with it can be catastrophic and irreversible.

### Why Leather Is Uniquely Challenging

Leather is animal skin that has been tanned to prevent decomposition. It retains the biological properties of skin — it pores, absorbs moisture, and reacts to environmental conditions. In Nairobi specifically, leather faces these threats:

- **Mould growth** during wet seasons — the warm, humid conditions of March through May are ideal for fungal growth on leather
- **Dry cracking** during the January–February dry spell — low humidity pulls natural oils from the leather
- **Dust accumulation** — fine Nairobi dust absorbs leather's surface oils, leaving it looking dull and lifeless
- **Colour transfer** — dark leather dye can transfer to lighter garments, especially when wet

### At-Home Leather Maintenance

**Regular cleaning (weekly):**

1. Wipe the jacket with a soft, dry microfibre cloth to remove surface dust.
2. For deeper cleaning, dampen a cloth slightly with distilled water (not tap water, which contains minerals that leave marks) and wipe gently.
3. Always air dry — never use a hair dryer, heater, or direct sunlight.

**Conditioning (monthly):**

1. Apply a small amount of leather conditioner or natural coconut oil to a soft cloth.
2. Rub in circular motions over the entire surface. This replenishes the natural oils that keep leather supple.
3. Allow it to absorb for thirty minutes, then buff off any excess with a clean cloth.
4. Do not over-condition — once a month is sufficient. Excess oil attracts dust and can darken the leather.

**Mould treatment (wet season):**

If you spot white or green mould on leather during the rains:

1. Take the jacket outdoors and wipe the mould off with a cloth dampened with equal parts water and white vinegar. The vinegar kills the mould spores.
2. Allow to air dry completely in a shaded area.
3. Apply leather conditioner once dry to restore any lost moisture.
4. Improve ventilation in your wardrobe — leave the door open periodically and consider a dehumidifier.

**Never do these things:**

- Never submerge leather in water — it absorbs moisture, loses shape, and may develop permanent water stains
- Never use soap, detergent, or household cleaners — they strip natural oils and cause cracking
- Never put leather in a washing machine or dryer
- Never hang leather on wire hangers — use wide, padded hangers that support the shoulders

### Leather Storage

- Store in a breathable cotton garment bag (never plastic — it traps moisture and encourages mould)
- Hang on padded hangers to maintain shoulder shape
- Keep away from direct heat sources
- During the long rains, check stored leather every two weeks for early mould signs

## 3. Suede Shoes and Accessories

Suede is leather with a napped (fuzzy) finish, and it is exceptionally popular in Nairobi for shoes, bags, and jackets. The napped surface that gives suede its distinctive look also makes it far more vulnerable to damage than smooth leather.

### The Nairobi Suede Challenge

Nairobi's red soil is the enemy of suede. The fine iron-oxide particles embed themselves in the nap and are extremely difficult to remove without damaging the texture. Rain is equally destructive — water causes suede to stiffen, darken, and lose its nap permanently.

### At-Home Suede Care

**After every wear:**

1. Use a suede brush (available from shoe shops across Nairobi for KSh 200–500) to brush the nap in one direction. This removes surface dust and restores the texture.
2. For light mud stains, let the mud dry completely, then brush off. Never wipe wet mud — it spreads and penetrates deeper.

**Water stain treatment:**

If your suede shoes get caught in a Nairobi downpour:

1. Stuff them with newspaper immediately to absorb moisture and maintain shape.
2. Allow them to dry naturally at room temperature — never near a heater, radiator, or direct sun.
3. Once dry, brush the nap with a suede brush.
4. If water marks remain, lightly dampen the entire shoe evenly with a mist of distilled water, stuff with newspaper, and let dry again. This eliminates the uneven water line.

**For stubborn stains:**

- A suede eraser (a special rubber block) can lift many stains without water
- White vinegar applied gently with a cloth can remove scuff marks — the vinegar evaporates without leaving a residue
- For red soil stains, brushing combined with a suede eraser is usually sufficient; professional cleaning is needed if the iron oxide has penetrated deeply

**Never do these things:**

- Never apply water directly to spot-clean suede — it creates water marks that are worse than the original stain
- Never use soap or detergent
- Never apply oil or conditioner — suede requires different care than smooth leather
- Never wear suede shoes during heavy rain season without waterproofing spray

### Suede Waterproofing

Before the rainy season, treat all suede items with a suede-specific waterproofing spray (available at shoe shops for KSh 300–600). Reapply after every cleaning or every ten wears, whichever comes first.

## 4. Wedding Gowns: Kenya's Most Precious Garment

Wedding gowns occupy a special category in garment care. They represent significant financial investment (often KSh 30,000–200,000+ in the Nairobi market), irreplaceable emotional value, and complex construction that makes them the most challenging items to clean and preserve.

### Why Wedding Gowns Need Special Attention

A typical wedding gown may combine:

- **Satin or silk base fabric** — vulnerable to water and heat
- **Lace overlay** — delicate and prone to snagging
- **Beadwork and sequins** — can loosen or discolour with improper cleaning
- **Boning and structured bodice** — can warp with water exposure
- **Multiple layers of tulle** — traps dust and body oils deep within the skirt

After a wedding, the gown typically carries: perspiration stains, makeup and foundation marks, food and drink splashes, dirt from the hem (outdoor weddings in Karen, Ngong Hills, or coastal venues are particularly prone), and invisible body oil deposits.

### Pre-Wedding Care

- Have the gown professionally steamed one week before the wedding — this removes storage creases and freshens the fabric
- Transport the gown in a breathable garment bag, not a plastic one
- Hang it in a clean, dry room away from cosmetics, hairspray, and perfume — all of these can stain fabric

### Post-Wedding: The Critical First Steps

**Within 48 hours of the wedding:**

1. Inspect the gown carefully under good light and note every stain, no matter how small. Invisible body oils will yellow over time if not removed.
2. Do not attempt to wash or spot-clean the gown yourself. The risk of making the situation worse is extremely high.
3. Hang the gown in a shaded, well-ventilated room. Do not fold it — folding creates permanent creases in structured gowns.
4. Bring it to a professional dry cleaner as soon as possible. The sooner a wedding gown is cleaned, the higher the success rate for stain removal.

### Long-Term Preservation

After professional cleaning, your wedding gown should be preserved for potential future generations:

1. **Acid-free tissue paper** should be used to pad the folds when storing — never use regular tissue, newspaper, or cardboard, as these contain acids that yellow fabric over time.
2. **An acid-free, archival-quality storage box** is the gold standard. These are available through specialty suppliers and some dry cleaners.
3. **Never store in a plastic bag** — plastic off-gasses chemicals that cause yellowing and fabric deterioration.
4. **Store in a climate-controlled area** — avoid attics (too hot), basements (too humid), and exterior walls (temperature fluctuations).
5. **Check the gown once every 12 months** — unfold, inspect for mould or discolouration, refold along different lines to prevent permanent creases.

### Preserving Kenyan Wedding Traditions

Many Kenyan couples also preserve their African attire — the groom's suits, the bride's kanga sets, and the various ceremonial outfits. These garments deserve the same level of care. Kitenge fabrics with wax-resist dyeing, kanga with printed text, and handwoven kikoy all have specific care requirements that professional cleaners understand and accommodate.

## When to Seek Professional Help

For all four of these fabric categories, professional cleaning is recommended:

- **At least once every six months** for regularly worn items (leather jackets, silk workwear)
- **Before and after each storage period** for seasonal items
- **Immediately after any staining event** on delicate fabrics
- **Before and after weddings** for formal attire
- **Whenever you are uncertain** — the cost of a professional assessment is far less than replacing a ruined garment
