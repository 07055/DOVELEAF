At our professional garment care facility in Nairobi, Kenya, we handle premium delicate fabrics daily. Here is the exact, universal framework we use to wash a suit without ruining the structure — the spot-cleaning, steam-freshening, and occasional full-wash method that maintains suit shape, prevents shoulder distortion, and extends the life of wool, polyester, and blend suits.

Suits are constructed with internal architecture — canvases, paddings, shoulder pads, and interlinings — that gives them their shape. Aggressive washing or improper drying can collapse this architecture, turning a tailored garment into a shapeless shell. The good news is that suits do not need frequent washing. Because they are worn over other clothing and typically for limited hours, they accumulate far less body oil and soil than t-shirts or undergarments. Most suits need full washing only once or twice per year. Between washes, proper maintenance keeps them fresh and presentable.

## The Maintenance Routine (Between Washes)

### Brush After Every Wear

Use a **horsehair garment brush** to brush the entire suit after every wear. Brush in downward strokes following the fabric grain. This removes surface dust, lint, and food particles before they settle into the fibres.

This single habit — taking thirty seconds to brush your suit after wearing it — will dramatically extend the time between washes and keep the fabric looking fresh.

### Hang Properly

Hang your suit on a **wide, shaped suit hanger** — the kind that supports the shoulder structure. Wire hangers or thin plastic hangers create shoulder bumps and distort the chest shape.

Button the front button when hanging to maintain the front shape. Leave the other buttons undone to reduce stress on the buttonholes.

### Air Out

After wearing, hang the suit in a **well-ventilated area** for twenty-four hours before returning it to the wardrobe. This allows body moisture and odours to evaporate. A bathroom with an open window works well, or a shaded outdoor area if direct sunlight is avoided.

### The Steam Refresh

The single most effective between-wash maintenance technique is **steaming**. A garment steamer releases warm moisture that:

- Kills surface bacteria that cause odour
- Relaxes wrinkles without the damage of iron pressing
- Refreshes the fabric nap and restores drape
- Removes light body odour

Hold the steamer fifteen to twenty centimetres from the suit surface and steam the entire garment, including the interior lining. Allow to dry naturally before wearing or storing.

## Spot Cleaning

For isolated stains — a food splash, a coffee drip, a sweat mark under the arm — spot cleaning is far safer than full washing.

### Fresh Stains

1. **Blot immediately** with a clean, dry cloth. Never rub — rubbing drives the stain into the fibres and spreads it.
2. **Dampen a clean cloth** with cold water and a tiny amount of mild detergent.
3. **Dab the stain gently** from the outside edges inward.
4. **Rinse** by dabbing with a separate cloth dampened in plain cold water.
5. **Blot dry** with a clean towel and hang to air dry.

### Sweat Stains Under the Arms

Sweat stains are the most common suit stain, especially in Nairobi's warm climate. The combination of sweat and deodorant chemicals creates a yellowed, stiff patch.

1. **Mix a paste** of one tablespoon baking soda, one tablespoon hydrogen peroxide (3%), and a small amount of water.
2. **Apply the paste** to the underarm area.
3. **Let it sit for thirty minutes.**
4. **Dab off the paste** with a damp cloth.
5. **Steam the area** to relax any residual stiffness.

### Ink and Oil Stains

Treat ink with isopropyl alcohol dabbed from the back (as with any garment). Treat oil with cornstarch absorption followed by dish soap dabbing. Always work from the back of the stain and never saturate the suit fabric.

## The Occasional Full Wash

Once or twice per year, or when the suit has accumulated general body soil that spot cleaning cannot address, a full wash is appropriate. But suits require a different approach than everyday garments.

### Option 1: Professional Dry Cleaning (Recommended)

For structured suits — those with canvas or fused interlinings — **professional dry cleaning is the safest option.** Dry cleaning uses solvent instead of water, which means:

- No risk of shrinkage
- No risk of interlining delamination (the glue that holds fused interlinings failing)
- No risk of shoulder pad distortion
- Minimal mechanical stress on the construction

Take your suit to a reputable dry cleaner and specify any stains or concerns.

### Option 2: Hand Washing (For Unstructured and Washable Suits)

Some suits — particularly unstructured linen suits, unlined summer blazers, and travel suits specifically labelled as machine washable — can be hand-washed at home.

1. **Fill a basin with cold water** (30 degrees Celsius maximum).
2. **Add a small amount of wool or delicate detergent.**
3. **Submerge the suit** and gently swish it through the water for two to three minutes. Do not scrub, twist, or agitate.
4. **Drain and rinse** by refilling the basin with clean cold water and swishing again.
5. **Remove excess water** by pressing the suit gently against the side of the basin. Never wring.
6. **Roll in a large towel** to absorb additional moisture.
7. **Lay flat on a drying rack** and reshape while damp — adjust shoulders, smooth the chest, straighten the sleeves.
8. **Allow to dry completely** at room temperature — typically overnight.

### Option 3: Machine Washing (Only for Labeled Machine-Washable Suits)

If the care label explicitly permits machine washing:

1. **Place the suit in a mesh laundry bag** to reduce mechanical stress.
2. **Select the delicate or wool cycle** with cold water.
3. **Use a small amount of liquid detergent.**
4. **Remove immediately** when the cycle ends.
5. **Lay flat to dry** — never hang a wet suit on a hanger.

## Dealing With Specific Suit Problems

### Shoulder Bumps

Shoulder bumps are caused by hanging a wet or damp suit on an improper hanger, or by the shoulder pads shifting during washing.

1. **Steam the shoulder area** generously with a garment steamer.
2. While still warm and slightly damp, **place your hand inside the shoulder** and gently smooth the bump outward.
3. **Hang on a proper suit hanger** and allow to dry completely.
4. For persistent bumps, professional re-shaping may be necessary.

### Faded Colour

Suit fabric can fade from UV exposure, friction, or repeated dry cleaning. To restore colour:

1. **Steam the suit** to open the fibres.
2. Apply a **fabric revitaliser spray** (available at dry cleaning suppliers) — these contain dye-depositing agents that refresh colour without altering the fabric.
3. For dark suits, a **fabric dye refresh** can be applied by a professional.

### Shine on Elbows and Seat

Shiny areas are caused by friction that has flattened the fabric fibres. This is common on wool suits where the elbows and seat experience regular contact.

1. **Steam the shiny area** generously.
2. **Drape a damp cotton cloth** over the shiny area.
3. **Press with a warm iron** (not hot — warm is sufficient) for a few seconds. The steam and pressure raise the flattened fibres.
4. **Brush with the horsehair brush** to restore the nap.

Nairobi's warm daytime temperatures and occasional rain mean suits in this environment face specific challenges:

- **Sweat accumulation is faster** — steam your suit after every wear during warm periods, even if you wore it for only a few hours
- **Dust is constant** — brush daily and store in a garment bag when not in use
- **Rain exposure** — if your suit gets wet in the rain, hang it to dry immediately in a well-ventilated area. Never leave a damp suit folded in a bag.
- **Colour fading from UV** — avoid wearing your best suit in prolonged direct sunlight; store away from windows

## The Care Label: Your Guide

The care label inside your suit tells you everything you need to know:

- **Dry clean only** — do not attempt home washing; this suit has a fused interlining that will delaminate in water
- **Dry clean** — dry cleaning recommended but hand washing may be safe for unstructured suits
- **Hand wash cold** — safe to hand wash at home with the method described above
- **Machine wash cold** — safe in a mesh bag on delicate cycle

When in doubt, **consult a professional dry cleaner** before attempting home cleaning. The cost of a professional assessment is far less than the cost of replacing a ruined suit.

## When to See a Professional

- **Suits with fused interlinings** (most modern suits) — dry cleaning only
- **Structural damage** — shoulder pad displacement, lining tears, or button damage
- **Severe staining** — wine, ink, or oil on expensive wool requires professional solvent treatment
- **Colour restoration** — professional re-dyeing for faded suits
- **Alteration after washing** — if washing causes slight shape changes, a tailor can re-block the suit
