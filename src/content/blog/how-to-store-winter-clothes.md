At our professional garment care facility in Nairobi, Kenya, we see a predictable pattern every year: customers bring in winter garments with small holes, mysterious threadbare patches, or fibre damage that appeared while the garments were supposedly safely stored. The culprit is almost always the same — **clothes moths** and their larvae, which feed on protein fibres like wool, cashmere, silk, and down while the garments sit undisturbed in wardrobes and storage boxes.

The tragedy is that this damage is almost entirely preventable. Moths do not attack clean fibres — they are attracted to **body oils, sweat residue, and food particles** on unwashed garments. A winter coat that is stored without washing is essentially a buffet for moth larvae. A winter coat that is washed before storage is largely invisible to them.

## The Golden Rule: Wash Before You Store

This is the single most important principle of off-season storage, and it is the one that most people skip: **every winter garment must be thoroughly cleaned before it goes into storage.**

Moths and their larvae are not attracted to the fibres themselves — they are attracted to the organic residue on the fibres. The sweat, skin oils, food splashes, and body odour that accumulate during wear are what draw moths. A freshly cleaned garment has virtually none of these attractants.

This means:

- **Wool sweaters** must be washed or dry-cleaned before storage
- **Cashmere items** must be hand-washed
- **Down jackets** must be washed and fully dried
- **Silk scarves** must be hand-washed
- **Even items that were worn only once or twice** must be cleaned — even minimal wear deposits body oils

**Do not store garments that are "probably clean enough."** Moths can detect organic residue at concentrations far below what humans can see or smell. If it has been worn, it needs cleaning.

## Step 1: Clean Every Garment

Follow the appropriate cleaning method for each fibre type:

- **Wool:** Hand wash in lukewarm water with wool detergent, or dry clean
- **Cashmere:** Hand wash with cashmere-specific detergent (see our cashmere care guide)
- **Down:** Machine wash on gentle cycle with down-specific detergent (see our down jacket guide)
- **Silk:** Hand wash with pH-neutral detergent and vinegar rinse
- **Synthetic fleece:** Machine wash on normal cycle

After cleaning, ensure every garment is **completely dry** before storage. Any residual moisture will promote mildew growth inside the storage container.

## Step 2: Inspect and Repair

Before storing, inspect each garment for:

- **Loose buttons** — repair or tighten before storage. A button that falls off in storage may not be noticed until next season.
- **Open seams** — re-stitch or take to a tailor. Small tears widen during storage as fabrics shift.
- **Missing hooks or zippers** — repair now rather than scrambling at the start of next season.
- **Existing moth damage** — if you find small holes or threadbare patches, treat the garment immediately before the damage spreads to neighbouring items.

## Step 3: Choose the Right Storage Container

The storage container is critical. Moths can enter through gaps as small as a few millimetres, and moisture buildup inside sealed containers promotes mildew.

### The Best Options

**Breathable cotton storage bags** — these are the gold standard. Cotton allows air circulation while blocking light and dust. Most importantly, cotton prevents the humidity buildup that sealed plastic containers create.

**Acid-free tissue paper** — wrap each garment individually in acid-free tissue paper before placing in the storage bag. This prevents colour transfer between garments and provides an additional barrier against moths.

**Clean cardboard boxes** — if fabric bags are not available, clean cardboard boxes work well. Line them with acid-free tissue paper and add cedar blocks.

### What to Avoid

- **Plastic bags or bins** — trap moisture and create a humid environment that promotes both mildew and moth activity. Vacuum-sealed bags are particularly problematic for protein fibres because the compression damages loft (especially down) and the airtight seal traps any residual moisture.
- **Wire or mesh containers** — provide no protection against dust and allow moth access from all sides.
- **Newspaper wrapping** — newspaper ink transfers to fabric and the acidic paper degrades protein fibres over time.

## Step 4: The Cedar and Lavender System

Cedar and lavender are natural moth deterrents that work without chemical pesticides. Their effectiveness comes from the strong aromatic compounds that moths find repellent.

### Cedar

Place **cedar blocks, cedar balls, or cedar oil sachets** in every storage container. Position them in direct contact with or within five centimetres of the stored garments.

Cedar oil is the active deterrent — it interferes with moth larvae's ability to locate and feed on protein fibres. The scent fades over time, so **refresh cedar blocks every six months** by lightly sanding the surface with fine sandpaper to release fresh oil.

### Lavender

Place **dried lavender sachets** alongside the cedar. Lavender provides a complementary deterrent effect and adds a pleasant fragrance to stored garments.

### Why Not Mothballs?

Mothballs contain **naphthalene or paradichlorobenzene** — synthetic chemicals that are effective moth killers but pose health risks:

- They emit toxic fumes in enclosed spaces
- They leave a persistent, unpleasant odour on garments
- They can cause discolouration on light fabrics
- They are particularly harmful to children and pets

Cedar and lavender provide comparable moth deterrence without these health risks.

## Step 5: Fold, Do Not Hang

**Fold all winter garments for storage** — do not hang them. Wool, cashmere, and knit fabrics are heavy, and hanging causes them to stretch under their own weight over months of storage. This creates permanent shoulder bumps, elongated hems, and distorted necklines.

Fold each garment neatly, wrap in acid-free tissue paper, and place in the storage container with cedar and lavender.

The exception is **structured coats with shoulder pads** — these can be hung on wide, shaped hangers inside breathable garment bags, as hanging preserves their structured shape better than folding.

## Step 6: Location, Location, Location

Store winter garments in a **cool, dry, dark, and well-ventilated location.**

- **Cool:** Heat accelerates fibre degradation. A cool closet or storage room is ideal.
- **Dry:** Humidity is the enemy of stored fabrics. If your storage area is humid, add silica gel packets to absorb moisture.
- **Dark:** UV light degrades protein fibres over time. Store away from windows and fluorescent lighting.
- **Well-ventilated:** Air circulation prevents the stagnant, humid conditions that moths and mildew prefer.

**Avoid:** attics (temperature extremes), basements (humidity), garages (temperature and moisture fluctuation), and under-bed storage in plastic bins (no air circulation).

## Monthly Storage Check

During the summer, check stored garments once per month:

1. **Open each storage container** and visually inspect for moth activity — look for small larvae (tiny white caterpillars), webbing, or new holes.
2. **Check for moisture** — feel the tissue paper and garments for any dampness. If found, remove and re-dry the affected items immediately.
3. **Refresh cedar** — if the cedar scent has faded, replace or sand the blocks.
4. **Air the garments** — remove from the container and hang in a shaded, ventilated area for a few hours to allow fresh air circulation.

This monthly check takes fifteen to thirty minutes and is the difference between undamaged garments and costly surprises next season.

## Nairobi-Specific Storage Considerations

Nairobi's climate is generally favourable for garment storage — the moderate temperatures and relatively low humidity (outside the rainy seasons) create conditions that are less moth-friendly than colder, damper climates. However:

- **During the long rains (March-May)** humidity increases significantly — add extra silica gel packets and check more frequently
- **Nairobi dust** is pervasive — use sealed containers (cotton bags with drawstrings) to prevent dust accumulation
- **Temperature fluctuations** between day and night can cause condensation inside sealed containers — choose breathable storage to prevent this
- **Clothes moths are present in Nairobi** — they are a global species and are found in virtually every urban environment
