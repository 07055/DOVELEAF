Here is the exact, universal framework we use to remove glue and adhesive stains from clothes and fabrics — the acetone method for super glue, the freezing technique for hot glue, and the peanut butter trick for sticker residue.

Glue stains are particularly frustrating because adhesive does not behave like a typical stain. It does not dissolve in water, it does not respond to standard detergent, and it creates a physical bond with fabric fibres that resists conventional washing. The good news is that every type of glue has a specific solvent that dissolves it completely. The key is identifying the glue type and choosing the correct solvent — using the wrong one can spread the adhesive, damage the fabric, or set the stain permanently.

## Identifying the Glue Type

Different glues require completely different removal approaches:

### Super Glue (Cyanoacrylate)

The fast-drying, industrial-strength adhesive. Super glue creates a rigid, transparent bond with fabric that feels like a hard plastic patch. It is solvent-resistant once fully cured — meaning you need to act before it hardens completely, or use a specific solvent after it has dried.

### White School Glue (PVA)

The washable, non-toxic glue used in schools. This is the easiest glue to remove because it is water-soluble when wet and remains soluble even after drying — it simply dissolves in warm water.

### Hot Glue (Thermoplastic)

The glue from a hot glue gun. Hot glue cools into a flexible, rubbery solid that sits on the surface of fabric rather than penetrating deeply. Removal relies on **temperature manipulation** — making the glue brittle enough to break free from the fibres.

### Fabric Glue

Designed specifically to bond fabric to fabric. Fabric glue penetrates deeply into fibres and creates a flexible, wash-resistant bond. This is among the most difficult adhesives to remove from fabric.

### Sticker Residue and Adhesive Tape

The adhesive from stickers, labels, and tape. These adhesives are typically rubber-based and sit on the fabric surface. Removal relies on **oil-based solvents** that dissolve the rubber adhesive.

## Super Glue Removal

### Fresh Super Glue (Still Wet or Tacky)

Speed is everything. Super glue begins curing within seconds and reaches full hardness within minutes.

1. **Blot — do not rub.** Rubbing spreads the glue and increases the affected area.
2. **Apply acetone** (nail polish remover — acetone-based, not acetone-free) to a cotton ball.
3. **Dab the wet glue** with the acetone-dampened cotton ball. The acetone dissolves the uncured cyanoacrylate immediately.
4. **Blot with a clean cloth** to lift the dissolved glue.
5. **Rinse with cold water** and launder as normal.

**Important:** Acetone dissolves some synthetic fabrics — test on a hidden area first, and never use on acetate, modacrylic, or rayon.

### Dried Super Glue

Once super glue has fully cured, the acetone method still works but requires more patience:

1. **Soak a cotton ball in acetone** and press it firmly against the dried glue spot.
2. **Hold for sixty to ninety seconds** — the acetone needs time to penetrate the hardened cyanoacrylate.
3. **Gently scrape** the softened glue with a dull knife or spoon edge. The glue should come away in soft, rubbery fragments.
4. **Repeat** if residue remains.
5. **Rinse thoroughly** with cold water and launder.

### The Vegetable Oil Method (Gentle Alternative)

For situations where acetone is unavailable or the fabric is too delicate:

1. **Apply a generous amount of vegetable oil** (cooking oil) to the dried super glue.
2. **Let it sit for fifteen to thirty minutes.** The oil penetrates the cyanoacrylate and weakens its bond with the fabric fibres.
3. **Gently rub the area** between your fingers — the glue should begin crumbling away.
4. **Wash with dish soap** to remove the oil residue.

This method is slower but safer for delicate fabrics.

## Hot Glue Removal

Hot glue's thermoplastic nature makes it uniquely responsive to temperature:

### The Freezing Method

1. **Place the garment in the freezer** for two to three hours. The cold makes the hot glue brittle and reduces its adhesion to fabric fibres.
2. **Remove the garment** and immediately **flex the fabric** at the glue point — bend it, fold it, and twist it gently. The brittle glue will crack and separate from the fibres.
3. **Peel away the cracked glue** pieces by hand.
4. **For remaining residue**, apply a small amount of rubbing alcohol and blot.

### The Heat Method (Alternative)

Alternatively, you can re-melt the hot glue to remove it:

1. **Place a brown paper bag** (or parchment paper) over the glue spot.
2. **Press with a warm iron** for five to ten seconds. The heat re-melts the glue, which absorbs into the paper.
3. **Lift the paper** and check — the glue should transfer to the paper.
4. **Repeat** with fresh paper until the glue is removed.

**Caution:** This method risks driving the glue deeper into the fabric if the iron is too hot. Use the lowest effective temperature and check frequently.

## White School Glue (PVA)

PVA glue is the simplest adhesive to remove:

1. **Soak the glued area in warm water** for fifteen to thirty minutes. PVA dissolves in water — even after it has dried.
2. **Gently rub the softened glue** between your fingers or with a soft brush.
3. **Launder as normal.**

For dried PVA that has been in the fabric for weeks: extend the soak to one to two hours. PVA remains water-soluble indefinitely, so patience is the only requirement.

## Sticker Residue and Adhesive Tape

Sticker adhesive is typically rubber-based and sits on the fabric surface.

### The Peanut Butter Method

Peanut butter contains oils that dissolve rubber-based adhesives:

1. **Apply a small amount of peanut butter** directly to the sticker residue.
2. **Let it sit for ten minutes.** The oils penetrate and dissolve the adhesive.
3. **Scrape away the residue** with a dull knife or your fingernail.
4. **Wash with dish soap** to remove the peanut butter and dissolved adhesive.

### The Cooking Oil Alternative

If peanut butter is unavailable:

1. **Apply vegetable oil, coconut oil, or olive oil** to the residue.
2. **Let it sit for ten to fifteen minutes.**
3. **Scrape and wash** as above.

### The Rubbing Alcohol Method

For residue that oil does not fully dissolve:

1. **Dab the residue** with a cloth dampened in rubbing alcohol.
2. **Let it sit for five minutes.**
3. **Blot and scrape** the softened adhesive.

## Fabric Glue Removal

Fabric glue is the most challenging adhesive because it penetrates deeply into fibres and is designed to be wash-resistant.

1. **Apply acetone** (if the fabric tolerates it) to the bonded area.
2. **Work the acetone into the fibres** using a soft brush for two to three minutes.
3. **Pull the bonded layers apart** gently while the acetone is dissolving the adhesive.
4. **Scrape away softened glue** with a dull knife.
5. **Repeat** as necessary — fabric glue typically requires multiple treatments.
6. **Wash thoroughly** with hot water and detergent to remove all solvent and adhesive residue.

For fabric glue on delicate fabrics, professional treatment is strongly recommended — the aggressive solvents required can damage silk, wool, and delicate synthetics.

## Glue on Specific Surfaces

### Carpet and Upholstery

1. **Scrape away excess glue** with a dull knife once it has hardened (for hot glue, freeze first).
2. **Apply rubbing alcohol** to the remaining adhesive and let it sit for five minutes.
3. **Blot with a clean cloth** — the dissolved adhesive will transfer to the cloth.
4. **Rinse** by blotting with a water-damp cloth.

### Denim

Denim tolerates all adhesive removal methods. For super glue on denim, the acetone method is most effective. Rinse with cold water to prevent indigo loss.

### Silk

Silk cannot tolerate acetone — it dissolves the protein fibres. For glue on silk:

1. **Freeze hot glue** and remove mechanically.
2. **Apply vegetable oil** to super glue or sticker residue — the oil method is the safest approach for silk.
3. **Professional treatment** is recommended for fabric glue on silk.

## Prevention

- **Protect work surfaces** during craft projects — lay down newspaper or a plastic sheet
- **Wear old clothes** during art and craft activities
- **Keep a bottle of acetone** (nail polish remover) in your craft area for immediate treatment of accidental super glue contact
- **Remove stickers and tape from garments immediately** — fresh adhesive residue is far easier to remove than aged residue

## When to See a Professional

- **Fabric glue on silk, velvet, or suede** — aggressive solvents required can damage these fabrics
- **Large-area adhesive contamination** — when glue has spread across a significant portion of a garment
- **Adhesive on embellished garments** — glue removal near beading, sequins, or embroidery requires professional precision
- **Adhesive on leather** — requires solvent-based treatment that preserves the leather finish

| Super glue (dried) | Acetone or vegetable oil | Soak and scrape | 15-30 minutes |

Every type of glue has a solvent that dissolves it — the challenge is matching the right solvent to the right adhesive. Identify the glue, choose the solvent, and treat before the glue has time to fully bond with the fabric fibres. Speed and the correct chemistry will handle virtually every adhesive situation.
