Down jackets are uniquely vulnerable to washing because their insulation depends on the **loft** — the ability of the down clusters to trap air and create warmth. When down gets wet, the clusters clump together and lose their loft. If dried improperly, the down remains clumped permanently, and the jacket becomes a flat, cold shell with lumpy patches. The key to washing a down jacket is understanding that the clumping is temporary and reversible — provided you dry the jacket correctly. With the right technique, your down jacket will emerge from washing with full, fluffy loft.

## The Pre-Wash Preparation

### Check the Shell Fabric

Before washing, inspect the jacket's outer fabric for tears, open seams, or broken zippers. Repair these first — the mechanical action of washing can turn a small tear into a large one.

### Close All Zippers and Fasteners

Close the main zipper, all pocket zippers, and any Velcro closures. Open Velcro will snag the shell fabric during the wash cycle and can cause tearing.

### Pre-Treat Stains

Down jackets are typically nylon or polyester shell fabrics, which are relatively stain-resistant. For visible stains:

1. **Dab with a cloth dampened in cold water and a small amount of mild detergent.**
2. For grease stains, apply a tiny drop of dish soap and let it sit for ten minutes before washing.
3. Rinse the pre-treated area with a damp cloth.

## The Washing Machine Method

Down jackets should be washed in a **front-loading washing machine** — not a top-loader with an agitator. The central agitator in top-loading machines can tear the shell fabric and damage the down clusters.

### Step 1: Load the Machine

Place the down jacket in the washing machine. You can wash one jacket at a time — overcrowding reduces cleaning effectiveness and increases mechanical stress.

### Step 2: Add Detergent

Use a **specialised down wash** (Nikwax Down Wash or Grangers Down Wash are widely available) or a **very mild liquid detergent** — one-quarter of the normal amount. Down-specific detergents are pH-neutral and formulated to clean without stripping the natural oils in down clusters.

**Never use:** fabric softener (coats the down and eliminates loft), bleach (damages down clusters), or powder detergent (can leave residue trapped in the down).

### Step 3: Select the Cycle

- **Water temperature:** Cold or warm (30 degrees Celsius maximum)
- **Cycle:** Gentle or delicate
- **Spin speed:** Low (400 to 600 RPM) or no spin
- **Extra rinse:** Select this option if available — any detergent residue will reduce loft

### Step 4: Run the Cycle

Allow the full cycle to complete. The jacket will emerge looking flat and lifeless — this is normal. The down clusters have clumped from the water, but this is entirely reversible.

## The Drying Process: Where Loft Is Restored

Drying is the critical stage. Improper drying leaves down permanently clumped. Proper drying restores full loft.

### Step 1: Remove Excess Water

After the wash cycle, **gently press the jacket against the side of the machine drum** to squeeze out excess water. Do not wring or twist — just gentle pressure.

### Step 2: The Tennis Ball Trick

This is the single most important technique in down jacket care:

1. **Place the damp jacket in the tumble dryer** on the **lowest heat setting.**
2. **Throw in two to three clean tennis balls** (or clean tennis shoes — the rubber soles serve the same purpose).
3. **Dry for fifteen-minute intervals**, removing the jacket to fluff and redistribute the down after each interval.

The tennis balls physically break up the clumps of down as they tumble through the jacket. Without them, the down dries in clumps and the jacket develops permanent lumpy patches.

**Do not use high heat.** Down clusters are protein-based and can be damaged by excessive temperature. Low heat is sufficient — the tennis balls provide the mechanical agitation, not the heat.

### Step 3: The Manual Fluff

After the first two or three dryer intervals:

1. **Remove the jacket** from the dryer.
2. **Use your hands to physically break up any remaining clumps** — feel through the shell fabric for hard lumps and massage them apart.
3. **Shake the jacket vigorously** — hold it by the shoulders and shake to redistribute the down.
4. **Return to the dryer** for another fifteen-minute interval.

### Step 4: Final Check

The jacket is fully dry when:

- No cold or damp spots remain when you press your hand against the shell
- The down feels uniformly soft and fluffy throughout
- There are no visible clumps or lumps

This process typically takes **one to two hours** of total dryer time on low heat.

## Restoring Water Repellency

Down jackets typically have a DWR (Durable Water Repellent) coating on the shell fabric that causes water to bead and roll off. This coating wears off over time and is stripped by detergent during washing.

### The Nikwax Treatment

After washing and drying, restore water repellency with a **Nikwax TX.Direct spray-on treatment**:

1. **Ensure the jacket is completely dry** before applying.
2. **Spray the entire outer shell** evenly with the Nikwax product.
3. **Wipe any overspray** from zippers, Velcro, and the interior.
4. **Allow to dry for twenty-four hours** before wearing or storing.
5. For maximum effectiveness, **tumble dry on low heat for twenty minutes** after the spray-on has dried — the heat activates the DWR treatment.

## Hand Washing Alternative

If a front-loading washing machine is not available:

1. **Fill a bathtub or large basin with lukewarm water** (30 degrees Celsius).
2. **Add down-specific detergent** — one tablespoon.
3. **Submerge the jacket** and gently agitate by pressing and releasing — like kneading dough. Do not scrub or twist.
4. **Soak for thirty minutes**, agitating gently every five minutes.
5. **Drain and rinse** by refilling with clean water and pressing the jacket through it. Repeat until no soap remains.
6. **Press out excess water** by rolling the jacket in a large towel and pressing.
7. **Dry using the tennis ball method** described above.

## When NOT to Machine Wash

Some down jackets should not be machine washed:

- **Jackets with welded seams** (no stitching — the fabric is heat-bonded) — the mechanical action can break the welds
- **Jackets with attached hoods or fur trim** — fur cannot be tumble dried and should be cleaned separately
- **Heavily soiled jackets** — if the shell is caked with mud or the down has absorbed body odour through the lining, professional cleaning is recommended
- **Vintage or delicate shell fabrics** — thin nylon shells may tear in the machine

## Storage

Store down jackets **loosely in a large cotton or mesh bag** — never in a compressed stuff sack for long-term storage. Compression damages the down clusters over time and reduces loft permanently.

A hanging garment bag is ideal for closet storage. Ensure the jacket has room to breathe and is not compressed against other items.

## Down Jacket Care in Nairobi

Nairobi's climate means down jackets are typically worn during travel or highland excursions rather than daily use. This means they accumulate less body soil but are more likely to need cleaning after specific incidents — a spilled drink at the airport, rain exposure on Mount Kenya, or dust from a safari trip.

For travel-stained down jackets, the spot-cleaning method is often sufficient — treating only the affected area rather than subjecting the entire jacket to a full wash.
