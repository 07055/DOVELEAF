## What Makes Raw Denim Different

If you are new to raw denim, the term refers to jeans made from indigo-dyed denim that has not undergone any pre-washing or distressing treatments after weaving. The fabric arrives stiff, dark, and uniform in colour. Over weeks and months of wear, the indigo dye — which sits on the surface of the cotton yarns rather than penetrating the core — gradually rubs off in high-friction areas, creating unique fade patterns that reflect your body shape and daily movements.

This is what makes raw denim special: **you are the artist, and your life is the brush.** Every knee crease, every wallet fade on the back pocket, every honeycomb pattern behind the knees — these are personal to you. No two pairs of well-worn raw denim look the same.

The challenge is that improper washing can destroy this art overnight. Indigo dye is soluble in water, and aggressive washing — hot water, tumble drying, excessive detergent — strips dye uniformly across the entire garment, erasing the contrast between faded and unfaded areas that gives raw denim its character.

## The Golden Rules of Raw Denim Care

Before diving into specific washing methods, internalise these five principles:

1. **Wash infrequently.** The more you wash, the faster you lose dye. Most raw denim enthusiasts wash their jeans every **three to six months** of regular wear — or only when they are visibly dirty or begin to smell.

2. **Use cold water.** Hot water accelerates indigo dye dissolution. Every wash should use water no warmer than **15–20°C (60–68°F)** — cold from the tap.

3. **Use minimal detergent.** Excess detergent strips dye faster. Use **one tablespoon** of a mild, dye-free liquid detergent per wash — far less than you would use for regular clothing.

4. **Never tumble dry.** The combination of heat and mechanical tumbling is the fastest way to destroy fade contrast. Always air dry.

5. **Wash inside out.** This protects the outer surface from abrasion against the washing machine drum and reduces dye loss on the visible face of the fabric.

## Method 1: The Bathtub Soak (The Purist's Choice)

This is the method preferred by raw denim purists worldwide. It provides the gentlest possible clean while maximising dye retention.

### What You Will Need

- A clean bathtub or large basin
- Cold water
- One tablespoon of mild, dye-free liquid detergent
- A clean towel
- A drying line or flat drying surface

### Step-by-Step

1. **Plug the drain and fill the tub** with enough cold water to fully submerge your jeans. The water should be cold — comfortably below room temperature.

2. **Add the detergent** and swish it around with your hand until it is fully dissolved. Do not create excessive suds — you want just enough surfactant to lift dirt and body oils, not enough to strip dye.

3. **Turn the jeans inside out** and submerge them. Press them down until they are fully under water.

4. **Soak for forty-five minutes to one hour.** During the first fifteen minutes, gently agitate the jeans every few minutes — swishing them back and forth in the water like you are hand-washing delicate lingerie. After the first fifteen minutes, let them soak undisturbed.

5. **Drain the tub and rinse.** Let the soapy water drain, then run cold water over the jeans to rinse. Continue until the water runs mostly clear — it will have a blue tint from loose indigo, which is normal and expected.

6. **Remove the jeans** and gently press them against the side of the tub to squeeze out excess water. **Do not wring or twist** — this stresses the cotton yarns and can create permanent distortion.

7. **Roll in a towel** to absorb more moisture, then either hang by the waistband on a padded hanger or lay flat on a drying rack.

8. **Air dry for 24–48 hours.** Keep them out of direct sunlight, as UV rays can bleach indigo unevenly.

### Why This Method Works Best

The bathtub soak introduces water and minimal surfactant without any mechanical agitation. The indigo dye that dissolves is limited to the loosest surface particles — the dye that would eventually rub off anyway. The contrast between your faded areas and the dark, unworn areas is preserved because the detergent does not have the mechanical assistance of a washing machine to strip dye from the unworn sections.

## Method 2: The Inside-Out Cold Machine Wash

This method is faster and more convenient than the bathtub soak, with only marginally more dye loss. It is the method we recommend for people who wash their raw denim more frequently.

### Step-by-Step

1. **Turn the jeans inside out** and fasten any buttons or zippers to prevent them from snagging the fabric.

2. **Place the jeans in the washing machine** — alone, or with only other dark-coloured items that you do not mind getting indigo dye transfer on. **Never wash raw denim with white or light-coloured clothing.**

3. **Select the delicate or gentle cycle** with cold water only. If your machine has a specific "cold" setting, use it. If it has a temperature dial, set it to the lowest possible position.

4. **Add one tablespoon of mild, dye-free liquid detergent** directly into the drum — not into the dispenser, which may distribute it too broadly.

5. **Select the shortest wash cycle available.** A fifteen-minute gentle cycle is ideal. A sixty-minute normal cycle will strip significantly more dye.

6. **Skip the spin cycle if possible.** High-speed spinning stresses the fabric and can create creasing that affects your fade patterns. If you cannot skip it, use the lowest spin speed available.

7. **Remove immediately** when the cycle ends. Do not let the jeans sit in the drum — the pooled water will continue dissolving dye.

8. **Hang or lay flat to air dry.** Same rules as the bathtub method: out of direct sunlight, 24–48 hours drying time.

### The Trade-Off

The machine wash introduces mechanical agitation — the drum tumbling the jeans against itself and the drum walls. This agitation strips slightly more surface indigo than the bathtub soak, which can subtly reduce the contrast between faded and unfaded areas. For most people, the difference is negligible, especially if you follow the gentle cycle and cold water rules.

## Method 3: The Spot Clean (For Small Stains)

Sometimes you spill something on your raw denim and need to address a specific spot without washing the entire garment. This is the approach we use at our facility for targeted stain treatment on raw denim.

### Step-by-Step

1. **Dampen a clean white cloth** with cold water.
2. **Apply a small amount of mild dish soap** to the damp cloth — not directly to the jeans.
3. **Dab the stained area gently** — do not rub. Press the cloth onto the stain, lift, and repeat. The soap will transfer to the stain through contact, not friction.
4. **Rinse by dabbing with a separate cold-water-damp cloth** to remove soap residue.
5. **Blot dry** with a clean towel and allow to air dry.

Spot cleaning confines dye loss to a tiny area and leaves the surrounding indigo completely untouched. It is the ideal method for minor spills and stains that do not warrant a full wash.

## The Freezing Method: Fact vs. Fiction

You will encounter advice online suggesting that placing raw denim in a freezer eliminates bacteria and odours without any water contact. Let us be direct about this: **freezing does not clean denim.**

Freezing does kill some bacteria, but it does not remove them from the fabric. When the jeans return to room temperature, the bacteria become active again within hours. Freezing also does not remove body oils, food stains, or any other physical soil.

At our facility, we view freezing as a **temporary odour management trick**, not a cleaning method. If you need your jeans to smell fresh between washes, hanging them in fresh air for 24 hours is significantly more effective than freezing them.

## How to Maintain Raw Denim Between Washes

The best way to keep your raw denim looking its best is to minimise the need for washing:

### Spot Clean Immediately

Treat spills and stains the moment they happen. Fresh stains are exponentially easier to remove than dried ones, and spot cleaning avoids a full wash cycle entirely.

### Air Out After Every Wear

Hang your jeans on a padded hanger or clothesline after each wearing. Allow them to air for at least eight hours before wearing again. This allows body moisture to evaporate and prevents odour-causing bacteria from establishing themselves.

### Sun-Cure for Freshness

A brief period of indirect sunlight — thirty to sixty minutes on a cloudy day or in a shaded area — provides natural UV sanitisation without significant indigo fading. This is particularly effective for eliminating odours.

### Rotate Between Pairs

Owning two or three pairs of raw denim and rotating between them gives each pair time to fully dry and air out between wearings. This dramatically extends the time between necessary washes and preserves the indigo longer.

## Understanding Indigo Loss: What Is Normal

Some indigo loss is inevitable and desirable — it is literally how raw denim develops its character. Here is what to expect:

- **First wash:** You will see the most dramatic blue water. This is loose surface indigo that was never fully bonded to the cotton. Its removal is expected and does not significantly affect the appearance of the jeans.
- **Subsequent washes:** The water will become progressively less blue with each wash. By the fifth or sixth wash, the water may run nearly clear.
- **Fade contrast:** After washing, your faded areas will appear slightly less bright, and your dark areas will appear slightly lighter. The overall contrast narrows slightly with each wash. This is why infrequent washing preserves the dramatic, high-contrast look that raw denim enthusiasts prize.

### The Denim Weight Factor

Heavier denim (14oz and above) retains indigo longer than lighter denim (under 12oz) because the thicker yarns have more dye to lose before the white cotton core is fully exposed. If you are choosing your first pair of raw denim, heavier weights are more forgiving of washing mistakes.
